The Stakes

Why the Packet Matters More Than You Think

A submittal packet is a recruiter's credibility on paper — sent to a hiring manager who will never meet the candidate before deciding whether to interview them.

01

One Error, One Lost Submission

A mismatched certification number or an inconsistent start date is often enough for a facility to reject a candidate outright — no questions asked, no second chance.

02

Compliance Has Deadlines

Licenses and certifications expire. A packet submitted with an expired credential — or one that will expire mid-contract — can disqualify a candidate before day one.

03

You Are the Last Line of Defense

The candidate doesn't proofread their own packet. The facility doesn't catch errors until it's too late. That review responsibility sits with you, the recruiter.

Think of it this way: the packet you submit today is the only version of your candidate the hiring manager will see until the interview. If it's sloppy, inconsistent, or incomplete, that becomes the candidate's reputation — even though it's really a reflection of the packet, not the person.
Document Anatomy

What's Inside a Submittal Packet

Most healthcare staffing packets are built from the same core documents, regardless of specialty. Here's what each one is, and why a facility requires it.

1

Submission Cover Sheet

The quick-reference summary a hiring manager scans first: contact info, availability, shift preference, certifications, and a snapshot of relevant experience.

Why it's required: it lets a busy hiring manager decide in 60 seconds whether to read further — accuracy here determines whether the rest of the packet even gets opened.
FIRST IMPRESSIONAVAILABILITYQUICK SCAN
2

Compliance / Clearance Screening Forms

Facility-specific or contract-specific screening questions — for example, a clearance form may ask whether the candidate has worked at a particular facility type, and if so, under what terms they left.

Why it's required: certain facilities and federal contracts carry legal or security obligations that go beyond standard credentialing — these forms document that the agency screened for them.
CLIENT-SPECIFICCOMPLIANCE
3

Resume / Candidate Profile

The full work history, skills summary, EMR/charting systems used, and detailed chronological experience — usually far more detailed than the cover sheet.

Why it's required: hiring managers need to verify specialty depth, recency of experience, and whether the candidate's background actually matches the unit they're hiring for.
WORK HISTORYSKILLS MATCH
4

Certifications & Licenses

Primary-source or candidate-provided proof of credentials — BLS/CPR cards, state licenses, specialty certifications — each with an issue date, expiration date, and unique certification number.

Why it's required: facilities are legally required to verify that anyone touching a patient holds active, unexpired credentials appropriate to their role.
LEGAL REQUIREMENTEXPIRATION-SENSITIVE
5

Government-Issued ID

A driver's license or equivalent, confirming legal name, date of birth, and address match what's listed elsewhere in the packet.

Why it's required: it's the anchor document — every other name, DOB, and address in the packet should trace back to this one without contradiction.
IDENTITY ANCHOR
6

Reference Checks & Self-Assessments

Structured evaluations from former supervisors covering communication, professionalism, dependability, and overall performance, plus candidate-completed skills self-assessments.

Why it's required: certifications prove a candidate can do the work in theory; references and self-assessments are the closest thing to proof they've done it well in practice.

Before You Hit Submit

Your Pre-Submission Checklist

This is the full field-level standard — cover sheet, resume, reference checks, and manual verifications — run through every single item, regardless of how confident you feel about the packet.

01 Cover Sheet Standards
NM

Candidate Name Format

Name is written as Firstname Lastname, with only the first letter of the first and last name capitalized — never all-caps, never last-name-first.

ADDR

Permanent Address

Filled in completely: street address, city, state, and ZIP code. No partial addresses (city/state only) go out on a cover sheet.

CONT

Contact Information

Both a phone number and an email address are present — not one or the other.

DT

Available to Start Date

Always written in mm/dd/yyyy format. The candidate's availability date must match the projected start date or fall before it — never after.

Y/N

Weekend / Holiday Availability

Recorded in simple Y/N format — not "sometimes," not "ask candidate," not left blank.

MIR

Credentials Mirror the Resume

Certifications, state license, education, and EMR experience listed on the cover sheet must be the exact same ones listed in the resume — no additions, no omissions.

REL

Previous Relevant Experience

The experience highlighted here should be the experience from the resume that most directly matches the job description — not just the most recent job by default.

PAST

All Experience Dates Are in the Past

Every date pulled from the resume must fall in the past. A future-dated typo on an experience entry (e.g., a transposed year) must be caught and corrected before submission.

NOTE

Additional Qualifying Notes

Any notes added should logically follow from the candidate's relevant experience and the job description — not generic filler that doesn't connect the two.

Summary format: the cover sheet summary should be written in bullet points, each one highlighting a specific reason this candidate is relevant to the job description — not a single paragraph of prose.
02 Resume Content & Format Standards

Every resume follows the same section order:

  1. Introduction table with candidate attributes
  2. Professional summary
  3. Skills
  4. Education
  5. Licenses and certifications
  6. Professional experience
EDU

Education Date Format

Written as Month, Year (e.g., "May, 2019") — and must match the education listed in the resume exactly.

LIC

License Must Not Be Expired

The license listed should not show as expired. If it has expired and the candidate is willing to renew it before or upon offer, that fact belongs in the selling points — not silently omitted.

JD

Most Recent Experience Mirrors the JD

The candidate's most recent role should be positioned to read as close a match as possible to the job description's requirements, for a genuinely strong fit.

SET

Hospital Setting Matches

Any hospital/facility setting referenced must match a setting the candidate actually worked in previously — never an assumed or aspirational setting.

1YR

Checklist Items Within the Last Year

Skills/competency checklist items extracted for the packet should be pulled from within the candidate's last year of experience.

GAP

No Unexplained Gaps

Every gap in the work history must be explained. Also check specifically for a gap between the license's received date and the candidate's first date of employment.

Every single professional experience entry needs all seven of the following. If any one is missing from the resume, flag it — don't fill it in with a guess:

  1. Facility name | City, State | Duration — duration written as Month, Year to Month, Year
  2. Job title
  3. EMR (electronic medical record system used)
  4. Position type
  5. Agency name
  6. Trauma level
  7. Facility type
03 Reference Check Standards
REF

Recency & Source

References are pulled from the candidate's previous 2–3 employers and must fall within the past 1.5 years — not from a job the candidate held five years ago.

=

Dates Match the Resume

The employment dates written on the reference check sheet for a given facility must be identical to the dates listed for that same facility on the resume.

04 Manual Verifications
INT

Available Interview Times

Format as HH:mm am/pm – HH:mm am/pm, Timezone (e.g., "10:00 am – 12:00 pm, EST") — always include the timezone.

SHFT

Shift Preference

Format as hh:mm am/pm – hh:mm am/pm (e.g., "7:00 pm – 7:00 am") — a time range, not a word like "nights."

Manual cross-check: on the employee reference check sheet, verify by hand that the dates of employment listed for each facility are the exact same dates written on the resume for that facility — this is a manual step, not something to assume is already consistent.
Golden rule: never read a packet document by document. Read it field by field, cross-referencing the same piece of information (a name, a date, a number) everywhere it appears. That's where almost every real error hides.
